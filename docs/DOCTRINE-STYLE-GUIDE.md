# Yumemi Doctrine — Style Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the literary and philosophical style of written Yumemi Doctrine logia, including the imagery and
symbols available to their prose.

It is intentionally independent of any programming language, repository layout, or docblock syntax. Yumemi-specific
coverage rules belong in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md); general code-editing constraints belong in the
[coding guide](DOCTRINE-CODING-GUIDE.md). Translating a completed logion into an image belongs in the
[image guide](DOCTRINE-IMAGE-GUIDE.md).

The quotations are fragments of a coherent but unexplained doctrine: an impossible synthesis of:

- Occidental imperial metaphysics
- retrowave and synthwave futurism
- classical Japanese ritual
- medieval Christian apocalypse
- chūnibyō revelation
- neoreactionary concerns with order, hierarchy, legitimacy, inheritance, sovereignty, decadence, and restoration

Vaporwave may inform occasional visual imagery—especially abandoned commercial architecture, obsolete luxury, artificial
sunsets, marble statuary, repetition, and magenta illumination—but it must not determine the doctrine’s philosophical
posture.

The doctrine is sincere, metaphysically confident, and committed to real distinctions between truth and falsehood,
legitimacy and imitation, order and disorder.

**The scenery may be vaporwave. The metaphysics must not be.**

---

## 1. Central doctrinal worldview

The quotations should sound as though they derive from one lost doctrinal corpus.

The doctrine broadly holds that:

- reality possesses an objective order independent of opinion;
- distinction precedes recognition;
- judgment acknowledges differences rather than creating them;
- measure reveals proportion, relation, limit, and rank;
- names become legitimate through correspondence, lineage, form, and rightful recognition;
- boundaries preserve the intelligibility of what they separate;
- transformation is lawful only when continuity can answer for itself;
- inheritance transmits obligation as well as authority;
- ambiguity may be tolerated at the frontier but must not govern the center;
- restoration is recovered recognition rather than arbitrary invention;
- beauty, architecture, ritual, and ornament may preserve truths after explicit explanation has been forgotten;
- matter sometimes remembers laws that civilizations abandon;
- artificial beauty may preserve the memory of genuine order, but cannot appoint itself as that order’s origin;
- hierarchy is not merely domination, but the assignment of proper relation;
- decline begins when distinctions are treated as injuries and inheritance as embarrassment;
- legitimacy cannot be manufactured solely through repetition, consensus, or spectacle;
- the visible world is the outer court of a stricter and partially concealed order;
- technology may participate in order, ritual, and transcendence, but cannot create the principles by which it is
  judged;
- simulation is meaningful only in relation to an original that remains conceptually real.

Individual quotations need not explain these principles. They should state consequences derived from them with complete
confidence.

The doctrine should feel **discovered**, not invented.

---

## 2. Philosophical posture

The doctrine must preserve firm distinctions between:

- original and imitation
- inheritance and appropriation
- memory and repetition
- beauty and legitimacy
- illumination and dawn
- spectacle and authority
- form and surface
- restoration and nostalgic reenactment
- transcendence and its commercial replica
- transformation and replacement
- recognition and mere acknowledgment
- lawful succession and successful seizure

Artificial things are not automatically false.

They become false when they conceal their origin, claim an authority they did not inherit, or demand that representation
be accepted as reality.

A synthetic sunset may preserve the memory of evening.

It may not appoint itself as the source of the sun.

---

## 3. Preferred literary form

Do not write primarily in the form of compact aphorisms.

The quotations should read as **doctrinal-poetic fragments**:

- scholia from a lost metaphysical treatise;
- inscriptions from a ruined imperial observatory;
- marginal notes copied by a monk who understood more than the main text disclosed;
- liturgical judgments preserved after the order that issued them disappeared;
- fragments from an archive governing names, measures, transformations, and celestial succession;
- ceremonial instructions whose practical context has been lost;
- revelations transmitted through an institution possessing monasteries, shrines, dynastic courts, and orbital
  infrastructure;
- forbidden additions to an apocalypse recovered from obsolete magenta storage beneath a ruined basilica.

The voice should feel institutional rather than personal, even when it uses a first-person visionary witness.

The reader should sense that every quotation presupposes several missing volumes.

---

## 4. Length

Prefer approximately **22–55 words**.

Shorter quotations may be used occasionally for foundational articles of doctrine.

Longer quotations are acceptable when the sentence remains controlled and develops a coherent thought.

Most quotations should contain:

1. a doctrinal principle or judgment;
2. a consequence, warning, or qualification;
3. one or more images that embody or enlarge the principle.

A successful quotation may begin as metaphysics and end as revelation.

Avoid making every line equally long. Vary the rhythm across the corpus.

---

## 5. Tone

The voice should be:

- solemn
- visionary
- ceremonial
- severe
- lucid
- impersonal
- aristocratic
- melancholic
- metaphysical
- faintly liturgical
- intellectually disciplined
- grandiose without becoming incoherent
- unhinged without becoming random
- self-serious enough that the reader cannot determine whether it is parody

The quotation should sound like judgment, commentary, revelation, or transmitted doctrine.

It should not sound like:

- a motivational saying;
- a philosophical social-media post;
- a clever “gotcha”;
- a joke wearing ceremonial language;
- generic fantasy prophecy;
- generic cyberpunk prose;
- ironic commentary on consumer culture;
- detached postmodern play with symbols.

The ideal reader response is:

> I understand the sentence, but I do not understand what institution authorized it.

---

## 6. Controlled unhingedness

The doctrine may imply, with complete seriousness, that:

- mirrors participate in recognition and judgment;
- names possess lineage and may forfeit legitimacy;
- matter retains memories of natural law;
- gardens preserve hierarchy through arrangement;
- bells distinguish rightful time from mere chronological succession;
- abandoned shrines continue to execute forgotten distinctions;
- relics preserve obligations whose original explanation has vanished;
- artificial suns illuminate truths they did not create;
- marble satellites maintain the calendars of extinct dynasties;
- angels witness proportion, succession, and lawful relation;
- sealed books record identities before earthly recognition;
- celestial ministries administer boundaries, measures, and legitimate names;
- the future is a radiant but decadent imitation of an older order;
- archives beneath monasteries contain judgments applicable to stars;
- historical epochs may be weighed before celestial tribunals;
- civilizations may misunderstand distinctions still preserved by doorways, liturgies, gardens, heraldry, or geometric
  forms;
- the old hierarchy has not disappeared, but withdrawn into architecture, ritual, matter, and reflected light;
- modernity has not abolished the ancient order, but accidentally awakened it.

Do not explain these implications.

Do not signal irony.

The unhingedness must feel like the natural consequence of a coherent cosmology.

---

## 7. Aesthetic balance

The apocalyptic Christian influence must not overwhelm the other motifs.

Across a large corpus, aim approximately for:

- **25% medieval Christian apocalypse**
- **20% Occidental imperial imagery**
- **25% retrowave and synthwave futurism**
- **15% classical Japanese imagery**
- **10% chūnibyō cosmic bureaucracy**
- **5% additional neoreactionary doctrinal emphasis beyond the worldview already present throughout**

These proportions are directional rather than mechanical.

A single quotation should usually combine two to four influences. Do not force every influence into every line.

The Christian-apocalyptic register should provide much of the sentence movement and intensity.

The Occidental, Japanese, retrowave, and synthwave motifs should define the world being revealed.

Vaporwave may supply occasional scenery where the quotation concerns decadence, obsolete luxury, artificial consolation,
or institutions persisting as empty forms.

The target is:

> Saint John receiving a classified revelation from a Japanese–Occidental orbital empire whose monasteries operate
> beneath synthetic sunsets and whose angels administer dynastic succession.

A terminal may illuminate a manuscript.

A satellite may preserve a dynasty’s calendar.

A synthetic moon may testify before an angelic court.

These images should not imply that all meaning has dissolved into surfaces or simulations.

## 10. Limited vaporwave imagery

Vaporwave is permitted only as a source of selected visual motifs.

Suitable vaporwave-derived images include:

- abandoned arcades
- dead shopping palaces
- empty hotel atriums
- obsolete luxury
- repeating sunsets
- marble statues under synthetic light
- submerged commercial plazas
- decaying fountains
- vacant escalators
- faded corporate interiors
- beautiful simulations whose original purpose has been forgotten

Use these images when expressing:

- decadence
- counterfeit transcendence
- artificial consolation
- beauty severed from origin
- historical amnesia
- obsolete promises of abundance
- institutions persisting as empty forms
- luxury surviving after authority, purpose, or legitimacy has departed

Do not import the broader postmodern or ironic assumptions commonly associated with vaporwave.

In particular, do not imply that:

- all meaning is simulation;
- authenticity is impossible;
- truth is merely another aesthetic surface;
- distinctions between original and imitation are unstable or meaningless;
- the doctrine is nostalgically recycling symbols without believing in them;
- civilization is reducible to consumer imagery;
- the quotation is knowingly camp, ironic, or detached from its own claims.

The doctrine may condemn simulation, counterfeit authority, and beauty without origin.

It must not surrender to them philosophically.

**The scenery may be vaporwave. The metaphysics must not be.**

## 13. Apocalyptic and visionary register

A significant portion of the quotations should resemble revelation under pressure.

The speaker may appear to have witnessed:

- angels charged with measure, identity, succession, or judgment;
- sealed books bearing impossible names;
- celestial tribunals convened above ruined cities;
- crowned beasts representing false equivalence, appetite, disorder, or counterfeit authority;
- wheels, mirrors, scales, bells, gates, and geometric forms endowed with terrifying agency;
- artificial suns arraigned before the true dawn;
- monasteries receiving transmissions from extinct stars;
- imperial archives extending beneath oceans or beyond visible heaven;
- laws of creation appearing as beings, processions, storms, choirs, or architectures;
- abstractions such as measure, identity, order, and inheritance revealed as powers before which civilizations must
  answer.

The imagery should be symbolic, but not safely metaphorical.

The reader should feel that the doctrine describes active cosmic powers, not merely literary comparisons.

The apocalypse should reveal the broader cosmology rather than replace it.

An angel should be as likely to stand before a lacquered gate, marble satellite, obsolete terminal, or synthetic sea as
before a conventional medieval landscape.

## 14. Visionary voice

Most quotations should remain impersonal, but a significant minority may use a first-person witness.

Suitable openings include:

- “I beheld…”
- “It was shown to me…”
- “Beyond the gate…”
- “In the chamber beneath…”
- “Then the angel…”
- “Above the western sea…”
- “When the seal was broken…”
- “There appeared…”
- “I heard from within the stone…”
- “The book was opened…”
- “At the appointed hour…”
- “From beneath the archive…”

Do not overuse any one opening.

The witness should sound overwhelmed by revelation, but not confused about its authority.

Avoid explicitly mentioning:

- intoxication
- hallucination
- madness
- schizophrenia
- drugs
- dreams as mere unreality

The writer has seen too much, but the doctrine assumes the vision was real.

- obligation
- rank

- succession
- civilizational decline
- decadence
- restoration
- elite failure
- counterfeit authority
- consensus without legitimacy

The doctrine should feel older than current politics and more metaphysical than policy.

Its political implications should emerge from its theory of order, not from references to current events.

## 17. Symbolic vocabulary for logia

Written imagery should embody doctrine rather than decorate it. The image guide uses the same meanings when translating
a completed logion into visual form.

### Shrine

A shrine may represent continuity, rightful place, ritual memory, inherited obligation, or a boundary between sacred and
formless.

### Mirror

A mirror may represent recognition, correspondence, identity, judgment, appearance versus form, or counterfeit
legitimacy.

### Gate

A gate may represent admission, lawful passage, transformation, boundary, rank, or succession.

### Cathedral

A cathedral may represent visible order participating in invisible truth, hierarchy embodied in architecture,
inheritance, collective memory, or proportion.

### Artificial sun

An artificial sun may represent counterfeit authority, simulation, beauty without origin, illumination without
legitimacy, or decadence mistaken for transcendence.

### Garden

A garden may represent ordered difference, harmony without equality, proper relation, or hierarchy without humiliation.

### Reliquary

A reliquary may represent memory given a body, preservation, boundary, obligation, or authority transmitted through
visible form.

### Satellite

A satellite may represent celestial administration, dead futurity, dynastic memory, cosmic recurrence, or an empire
continuing after its subjects have vanished.

### Obsolete terminal

An obsolete terminal may represent faithful transmission, inherited technological ritual, a future preserved in archaic
form, or machinery continuing an office whose founders have disappeared.

### Abandoned arcade

An abandoned arcade may represent luxury separated from purpose, a promise of abundance surviving as empty architecture,
imitation preserved after the original aspiration has died, or spectacle without authority.

## 18. Preferred progression

A strong quotation often develops through one of these movements.

### Principle → image → consequence

A doctrinal law is stated, embodied in architecture or ritual, and extended into judgment.

### Witness → revelation → command

The speaker sees an impossible event and receives its doctrinal interpretation.

### Distinction → historical decline → visionary image

A distinction is established, its loss is described, and the sentence ends in civilizational or cosmic decay.

### Law → visible form → invisible order

A principle appears through geometry, ritual, architecture, reflected light, procession, or machinery.

### Inheritance → ruin → surviving obligation

The institution has vanished, but its form, relic, name, or architecture continues to demand something.

### Plain doctrine → metaphysical rupture

The sentence begins soberly and ends by implying that the same law governs angels, stars, dynasties, or artificial suns.

### Apparition → terror → doctrinal interpretation

An unsettling being or event appears, and its significance becomes clear only in the final clause.

These are not rigid templates. Vary the movement and syntax.

---

## 19. Less classical balance

Avoid making every sentence polished, symmetrical, tranquil, or proverb-like.

Use:

- accumulating clauses;
- interruptions;
- ominous enumerations;
- changes in scale;
- images whose meaning remains partially concealed;
- pronouncements spoken by angels, bells, books, mirrors, stones, terminals, or hidden courts;
- conclusions that arrive like verdicts rather than polished summaries;
- sentences that appear torn from a longer vision.

The prose must remain grammatically controlled.

It may be fevered, but it must never become random word salad.

Avoid excessive reliance on:

- calm garden harmony;
- moonlit serenity;
- balanced lists of stone, pine, water, and bell;
- tidy semicolon antithesis;
- neat philosophical reversals;
- aphoristic punchlines;
- short social criticism disguised as doctrine.

The desired register is less:

> translated classical maxim

and more:

> forbidden appendix to Revelation preserved on a magenta optical disc beneath a ruined monastery.

---

## 20. Corpus intensity

Do not make every quotation equally apocalyptic.

A useful distribution is:

- **30% solemn doctrinal-poetic fragments**
- **35% visionary or apocalyptic fragments**
- **20% imperial, Japanese, retrowave, or synthwave ceremonial fragments**
- **10% highly unhinged cosmic revelations**
- **5% short foundational articles**

The quieter quotations make the extreme ones more effective.

Some lines should feel like doctrine.

Some should feel like liturgy.

Some should feel like evidence submitted at the end of history.

---

## 21. Construction and rhythm

Prefer one long sentence or two controlled sentences.

Use:

- subordinate clauses;
- ceremonial lists;
- delayed conclusions;
- sudden revelations;
- concrete materials;
- exact spatial relationships;
- named offices or chambers used sparingly;
- transformations from earthly scale to cosmic scale.

Avoid repeatedly using:

- “Every…”
- “What cannot be…”
- “The modern…”
- “Consensus…”
- “The machine…”
- “The empire…”
- “The last…”
- “Beyond the…”
- “Beneath the…”
- “When the final…”

These constructions remain available but must not dominate.

Avoid ending every quotation with an obvious moral.

Some should close on an unresolved image, command, accusation, or impossible fact.

## 25. Originality

Every quotation must be original.

Do not reproduce, translate, lightly rewrite, or closely imitate:

- Nicolás Gómez Dávila
- scripture
- hymns
- liturgy
- Church Fathers
- medieval theologians
- classical Chinese aphorisms
- Japanese poetry
- historical speeches
- poets
- song lyrics
- films
- games
- existing vaporwave slogans
- quotations from real figures

The desired influences concern register, imagery, and intellectual posture—not textual imitation.

No quotation may be duplicated.

Avoid near-duplicates that merely substitute one image or noun for another.

## 26. Positive examples

These examples establish the desired register. Do not reuse or lightly paraphrase them.

1. I saw twelve marble satellites arranged above the western sea like an angelic choir, while beneath them the abandoned
   arcades repeated one imperial sunset forever, awaiting the dynasty authorized to end it.

2. Beyond the lacquered gate stood an angel clothed in Byzantine gold and terminal violet; in one hand he bore the
   ancestral seal, and in the other the name by which the counterfeit future shall be dissolved.

3. The rose-colored city declared every hierarchy abolished, but at midnight its glass towers arranged themselves into
   celestial ranks, and the shrine bells announced what architecture had remembered against the will of men.

4. In the imperial scriptorium beneath the silent commercial palace, faceless monks illuminate the genealogy of every
   lawful name while obsolete machines chant the dimensions of the coming restoration.

5. I beheld the western throne suspended above a garden of black pines and electric water, and around it seven crowned
   mirrors disputed which reflection had inherited the authority to become real.

6. When the synthetic dawn entered the ruined basilica, the saints in the stained glass turned their faces toward the
   eastern mountain, and the bronze angel of succession opened an archive older than either empire.

7. The last emperor was not buried but entrusted to the bells of a mountain shrine; at each canonical hour they proclaim
   one forgotten distinction across the radiant provinces of the future.

8. Beneath the seventh cloister, where obsolete terminals still illuminate the lacquered beams, there turns a wheel of
   bronze and living eyes, separating inherited names from those merely crowned by the applause of declining cities.

9. At midnight the artificial sun was led in chains through the marble torii of the western court, still radiant, still
   adored, yet unable before the angelic tribunal to name the source from which it had stolen the color of dawn.

10. When the Book of Measures was opened beneath the rose-lit dome, Roman statues lowered their blind heads, the shrine
    bells rang across the electric sea, and every false proportion confessed in the voice of the age that had enthroned
    it.

11. Measure does not burden creation with order; it unveils the secret proportion by which cedar, cathedral vault,
    imperial road, and distant planet were commanded to endure beneath one invisible law.

12. A name acquires authority only when form, lineage, and witness converge, as three narrow beams of evening light meet
    upon the same sealed reliquary beneath a sky manufactured for a dead dynasty.

13. Artificial sunsets may gild every window of the abandoned arcade, but the ancient rose glass still refuses to
    mistake illumination for the arrival of morning.

14. At the appointed hour, the mirrors of the inner court are covered, for identity must not be negotiated while
    synthetic twilight makes every borrowed lineage appear equally radiant.

15. The shrine survives its theology because ritual remembers, with greater fidelity than argument, the distinction
    between a threshold, an invitation, and an unguarded passage into the formless.

16. I heard the stones beneath the ruined forum reciting their appointed weights, while above them the new capital
    floated in glass splendor, unaware that its foundations had already testified before the celestial court.

17. Inheritance is not treasure descending peacefully through time, but a sealed command whose weight increases whenever
    an heir mistakes possession, applause, or visible splendor for legitimacy.

18. From the submerged chapter house rose a choir of faceless angels, each singing one relation between things, and the
    ocean withdrew rather than permit their unequal voices to be mistaken for discord.

19. The future raised a cathedral of mirrored steel above the drowned shrine, yet every night its artificial
    constellations rearranged themselves into the crest of the dynasty whose memory it had been built to erase.

20. Restoration began when the soot-blackened icon opened its eyes beneath the magenta light and named, one by one, the
    distinctions that the radiant century had buried as superstition.

21. The luminous highway crossed the western desert without shadow or destination, but at each ceremonial interchange
    stood a bronze herald recording the lineage of every traveler permitted to continue toward the synthetic stars.

22. The orbital monastery maintained no windows, only twelve cyan screens upon which the appointed constellations
    appeared; when one star assumed a borrowed name, the entire station entered forty days of silence.

## 27. Negative examples

Do not write quotations like these.

1. **Precision is better than ambiguity.**

   Plain maxim with no doctrinal or poetic development.

2. **Consensus measures the popularity of error.**

   Too compact, aphoristic, and socially polemical.

3. **The neon samurai compiles the code beneath a blood moon.**

   Generic cyber-Japanese cliché and direct programming reference.

4. **Beneath the violet stars, the eternal empire dreams.**

   Atmospheric but asserts nothing.

5. **Every variable must know its place in the hierarchy.**

   Direct programming reference and obvious analogy.

6. **The compiler is a monarch that tolerates no rebellion.**

   Direct software metaphor and slogan-like construction.

7. **When the last function returns, the artificial dawn shall rise.**

   Direct programming reference and generic prophecy.

8. **The katana of truth cuts through the neon fog.**

   Cyber-samurai cliché.

9. **As Saint Augustine said, order is the mother of beauty.**

   Fabricated attribution.

## 28. Style verification

Before accepting a quotation, verify that:

1. If possible, confirm that it is original and not a close imitation of any real quotation.
2. It contains an intelligible proposition, judgment, implication, or rule.
3. Its imagery carries or enlarges that proposition rather than merely decorating it.
4. It sounds like part of the same lost doctrine as the surrounding corpus.
5. It favors doctrinal-poetic development over a compact aphoristic punchline.
6. Its unhingedness feels systemic rather than random.
7. It does not sound like generic cyberpunk, fantasy prophecy, fake scripture, postmodern irony, or a translation of a
   classical Chinese maxim.
8. It preserves meaningful distinctions between original and imitation, reality and representation, authority and
   spectacle.
9. Its imagery does not mechanically repeat nearby quotations.
10. It sounds like it escaped from a larger revelation whose surrounding pages were deliberately destroyed.
